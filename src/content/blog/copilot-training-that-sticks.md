A team we spoke with had already run an internal Microsoft Copilot training session. It didn’t stick. They reached out for professional help because they needed more than another tour of the tool: they wanted people to use it in their day-to-day work.

That’s the distinction worth planning for. A training session can introduce Copilot. Adoption means that when a familiar task comes up next week, someone knows when to reach for Copilot, what to ask it, and how to check the result.

## Give people a reason to use it again

1. **Start with one recurring task per role.** An operations coordinator might draft a weekly status update from approved notes. A manager might turn meeting notes into an action list, then verify the owners and due dates. Pick work people already do, not an impressive demo they’ll never repeat.
2. **Have people practice during training.** Give everyone a safe example, let them try the task themselves, and compare the draft with what they would actually send. Make clear which information is approved for use and what still needs human review.
3. **Come back after they’ve tried it at work.** Use a short follow-up or office hour to ask what they reused, where they got stuck, and what needed fixing. Give someone on the team ownership of the follow-up so the habit doesn’t depend on the trainer.

## Measure what happened after the session

Attendance tells you who showed up. A better first check is whether people used Copilot again on the task they practiced, whether the draft was useful after review, and what would stop them from using it next time. Even a short check-in with the pilot group can tell you what to teach next.

If you’ve already held a Copilot session and little changed, don’t start by booking the same class again. Pick one workflow, give people a chance to practice it, and check back after they’ve tried it on their own.
